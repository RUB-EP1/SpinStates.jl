3. Boosts

Boosts act on a SpinState the same way — Bz(s, γ) (parameterised by $\gamma=\cosh\xi$) moves the carrier momentum and the coefficients together. Under boosts the roles from Tutorial 2 swap over.

Helicity is invariant under a collinear boost

Helicity is the spin along the momentum; a boost along that same direction does not turn the momentum, so nothing happens to the coefficients — not even a phase:

pz = FourVector(0.0, 0.0, 0.5; M = 0.3)          # momentum along +z
sh = spin_state(Helicity(), pz, normalize(ComplexF64[0.6, 0.8]))

Bz(sh, 1.8).coeffs ≈ sh.coeffs                    # boost along +z: unchanged
true

This is the property that makes helicity the convention of choice for the sequential boosts down a decay chain.

A non-collinear boost rotates a canonical spin (Thomas–Wigner)

Boost a particle whose momentum is not along the boost axis and a canonical state picks up a genuine rotation — the Thomas–Wigner rotation. Take a particle moving along $+\hat x$ and boost it along $+\hat z$:

px = FourVector(0.5, 0.0, 0.0; M = 0.3)          # momentum along +x
sc = spin_state(Canonical(), px, ComplexF64[1, 0])  # pure |+1/2⟩ along lab ẑ

sc2 = Bz(sc, 1.8)
sc2                                               # the lower component is now populated

\[|\psi\rangle_{\mathrm{c}} = 0.957\,\left|\tfrac{1}{2},+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rangle - 0.29\,\left|\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rangle\]

The mean spin $\langle S\rangle$ tilts in the $xz$-plane by the Wigner angle:

(spin_expectation(sc), round.(spin_expectation(sc2); digits = 4))
([0.0, 0.0, 0.5], [-0.2772, 0.0, 0.4161])

A boost is not a rotation, yet a canonical state responds to one as if rotated; a helicity state in the same situation would only rephase. That "boost that acts like a rotation" is exactly the Wigner rotation of the next tutorial, where we extract its axis and angle.

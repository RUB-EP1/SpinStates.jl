2. Rotations

SpinStates extends the FourVectors transforms Rx, Ry, Rz, Bz so they act on a SpinState as well as on a four-vector. Applying one moves the carrier momentum and the spin coefficients together:

p0 = FourVector(0.4, -0.2, 0.3; M = 0.4)
s0 = spin_state(Canonical(), p0, normalize(ComplexF64[0.6, 0.8]))

s1 = Ry(s0, 0.6)     # rotate by 0.6 about ŷ; equivalently  s0 |> Ry(0.6)
s1

\[|\psi\rangle_{\mathrm{c}} = 0.337\,\left|\tfrac{1}{2},+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rangle + 0.942\,\left|\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rangle\]

The two bases respond very differently to such a pure rotation $R$ — this is the cleanest way to tell them apart.

Canonical: the spin follows, $\text{coeffs}\to D(R)\,\text{coeffs}$

The canonical boost $u_C(p)=R\,B_z\,R^{-1}$ is covariant ($u_C(Rp)=R\,u_C(p)\,R^{-1}$), so the rotation the coefficients see is $R$ itself and they transform by $D^s(R)$ — independent of the momentum. For spin $\tfrac12$ that Wigner $D$-matrix is just the $SU(2)$ matrix Urot:

β = 0.6
Ry(s0, β).coeffs ≈ Urot([0, 1, 0], β) * s0.coeffs
true

This is why canonical states are the natural home for ordinary angular-momentum algebra: a rotation acts on the spin index and nothing else.

Helicity: helicity is conserved (only a phase)

Helicity is the spin along the momentum. Under a rotation the momentum turns, and the massive-particle little group carries the spin along with it — a rotation about the momentum. Each helicity component therefore only picks up a phase; the magnitudes (the probabilities of $\lambda=+\tfrac12$ vs $-\tfrac12$) are frozen:

sh = spin_state(Helicity(), p0, normalize(ComplexF64[0.6, 0.8]))
sh1 = Ry(sh, β)

abs.(sh1.coeffs) ≈ abs.(sh.coeffs)     # helicity is conserved
true
sh1.coeffs ./ sh.coeffs                # …the change is a pure phase per component
2-element Vector{ComplexF64}:
 0.9918515428056207 + 0.1273990464411325im
 0.9918515428056207 - 0.12739904644113254im

So under rotations: canonical mixes (by $D(R)$), helicity only rephases. Both preserve the norm and both agree with the rigid rotation of $\langle S\rangle$ from Tutorial 1. The precise rotation the coefficients see — and how to read off its axis and angle — is the subject of Tutorial 4.
